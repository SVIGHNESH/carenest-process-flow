Ask the nurse first, then take the money

Right now the plan takes the family's money first, then waits for someone in the office to approve, and only then tells the nurse. We suggest doing it the other way round. Here's why, in plain words.

A note on UPI. Most families will pay by UPI. UPI can't reserve money the way a card can: the money leaves the family's account at checkout. If no nurse says yes, Razorpay sends it back automatically, but it can take a few days. So the "finding a nurse" wait should stay short. CareNest can also ask Razorpay whether UPI one-time mandates, which block the money without taking it, are available on its account.
  1. At checkout the family's ₹1,000 is held, not confirmed. On a card it stays in their account as a reserved amount. With UPI it leaves and comes back by itself if no nurse says yes.
  2. When a nurse says yes the payment is confirmed. Razorpay keeps its fee and sends the rest to CareNest's bank account.
  3. After the visit CareNest pays the nurse their share. CareNest sets the rate and how often nurses are paid, e.g. weekly.
  4. If the family cancels the cancellation rule below decides how much goes back to them and how much goes to the nurse for their time.

Six problems, and the simple fix for each

Why today's plan hurts families, nurses and CareNest.

Families pay for visits that may never happen

If the office says no, the family waits 5–7 days for a refund, and the payment company keeps its fee (about 2%).

Fix: hold the payment at checkout. Confirm it only when a nurse says yes.

Someone must approve every booking, day and night

Bookings are for the next day, so a request at 11 pm needs someone awake. While it waits, the nurse can't be booked by anyone else.

Fix: the office only steps in when no nurse says yes within an hour.

The nurse is never asked

The nurse finds out by text after the family was told "confirmed". If they're sick or miss the text, nobody turns up.

Fix: the nurse taps Accept or Decline. Only then is the family told.

The nurse the family picked can be swapped

The family chooses a nurse and pays, then the office may send someone else. That feels like a broken promise.

Fix: call it a "preferred nurse". If someone else comes, tell the family before they pay.

Multi-day care is charged as nonstop care

A 7-day booking is charged as 168 hours with one nurse, around the clock. No nurse can do that.

Fix: sell daily shifts, like 8 or 12 hours a day.

A cancel at the last minute costs the nurse

Families get a full refund until the visit starts, even if the nurse is already at the door.

Fix: a clear cancellation rule shown before paying. See the suggested rule.

Time limits in the better plan

Nothing waits forever.

  • 15 minfor the family to finish paying, or the booking is dropped
  • 15 minfor each nurse to answer, or the next nurse is asked
  • 1 hourwithout a yes, and the office is alerted to help
  • 3 hoursbefore the visit with no nurse: held payment returned, family told

Suggested numbers. CareNest can change them.

How much comes back when a family cancels

Bookings are for the next day, so the time windows are short. The later the cancel, the more of the nurse's day is lost, so the less comes back.

Family cancelsFamily gets backNurse gets
Before a nurse says yes100%Nothing, no work was promised
More than 12 h before the visit100%Nothing, enough time to fill the slot
3 to 12 h before50%A share of the 50% CareNest keeps
Under 3 h, or nurse already on the way0%A fixed visit fee for the lost time
After the visit has started0%Paid in full

When CareNest or the nurse cancels

  • Nurse cancels or doesn't turn up: 100% back to the family, plus a credit for their next booking. The nurse gets a mark on their record.
  • No nurse found: the held payment goes back by itself.
  • CareNest cancels for any reason: 100% back.

Special cases

  • Multi-day care: any future day can be cancelled with 24 h notice for a full refund of that day. The next day follows the table above.
  • Emergencies (the patient is taken to hospital, or passes away): the office can give a full refund whatever the timing, and writes down why.

Razorpay keeps its fee on the original payment when money is refunded, so each refund after a nurse said yes costs CareNest that fee.

  1. Before paying the family sees the cancellation rule on the review screen and ticks a box to agree.
  2. On the cancel screen the app shows the exact amount coming back before the family confirms.
  3. The refund starts by itself. Nobody in the office has to press a button. The family can see its status: started, done, or failed.
  4. If a refund fails, the office is alerted and sorts it out by hand.

What changes for each person

The same three people as in the simple overview.

Family

Signs in with one phone code. Never loses money on a visit nobody agreed to. Gets a clear "Confirmed" with the nurse's name.

Nurse

Gets a notification and chooses to accept or decline. Starts the visit with a code from the family, so the visit is on record.

CareNest team

Sees every booking live but only acts when something is stuck. Can still assign a nurse by hand at any time.
